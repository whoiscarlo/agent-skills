# Offer Crafting

A playbook for building offers people feel stupid saying no to. Distilled from
Alex Hormozi's *$100M Offers* (read in full), the attraction/continuity chapters of
*$100M Money Models*, Tim Luong's 5-step killer offer structure, and 2026 field
research. This is a decision toolkit, not a summary. Start at the checklist that
matches your task.

**Where this fits among sibling skills:** this skill designs *what you sell and how
it's framed*. For the funnel that delivers it, see `lead-gen-funnels`. For the words
on the page, see `conversion-copy`. For the psychology of why buyers act, see
`sales-psychology`. For in-product nudges, see `ux-persuasion`. For unit
economics, pricing math and churn, see `business-diagnosis`. For naming a
brand or product (not an offer), see `brand-naming`.

## 1. The value equation (use before touching price)

```
        Dream Outcome  ×  Perceived Likelihood of Achievement
Value = ────────────────────────────────────────────────────
        Time Delay     ×  Effort & Sacrifice
```

- Increase the top two, shrink the bottom two. If the bottom hits zero
  (instant result, no effort), value is effectively infinite.
- **Perception is reality.** London Underground raised satisfaction more with a
  countdown map than with faster trains. Communicate the improvement or it
  doesn't exist for the buyer.
- Beginners inflate the top (bigger claims, which are easy, lazy, and what everyone does).
  Veterans compete on the bottom: faster and easier wins. "The only thing that
  beats free is fast." If you compete against free, sell speed.
- Time delay has two parts: the long-term dream (what they buy) and short-term
  wins (why they stay). Engineer an emotional win within days of purchase.

### Value equation worksheet

For the offer you're auditing, write one line each:

1. Dream outcome, in the customer's words (the vacation, not the plane flight).
2. Why they'd doubt it will work *for them* (kills perceived likelihood).
   Proof that answers each doubt.
3. Time to first felt win. Can you cut it to minutes?
4. Everything it costs them besides money: setup, learning, habit change,
   embarrassment. Which can the product absorb (done-for-you) instead?

Score each 0 to 1 like Hormozi's meditation-vs-Xanax table. Anything at 0 is why
people aren't buying.

**Perceived likelihood is usually the weakest lever.** Buyers want the result.
They do not believe you can deliver it. If the honest facts are not
compelling, do not stretch the claim. Change reality first: get more results
and more reviews, then state them plainly. (Why Don't People Buy? Because
Nobody Believes You)

## 2. Preconditions: market and niche

An offer cannot outrun a bad market. Check before crafting:

- **Four market indicators:** massive pain, purchasing power, easy to target,
  growing. Order of leverage: starving crowd > offer strength > persuasion skill.
- **Niche down and commit.** "Time Management" sells for $19; "Time Management
  for B2B Outbound Power Tools & Gardening Sales Reps" sells for $1,997: same
  material, 100x price via specificity. Both books and Luong agree: the tagline
  must make the ideal customer say "this is exactly for me."
- Iterate the niche from data: after 5 to 10 customers, redefine the ideal customer
  profile from who actually paid, stayed, and got results (Luong's loop:
  re-run the whole structure every 6 to 12 months).

## 3. Grand Slam Offer construction (the 5-step stack)

1. **Dream outcome.** State the destination, not the vehicle.
2. **List every problem** between them and it. Walk their timeline: before,
   during, after each step. Each problem has four flavors mapping to the value
   equation (not worth it / won't work for me / too hard / too slow). 30 to 60
   problems is normal; each one is a value opportunity, and one unsolved
   objection can block the sale.
3. **Flip problems into solutions.** "Buying healthy food is confusing" →
   "How anyone can buy healthy food fast, easily, cheaply."
4. **Generate delivery vehicles** per solution using the Delivery Cube:
   one-on-one / small group / one-to-many; DIY / done-with-you / done-for-you;
   live vs recorded; response speed; and the 10x / 1-10th test (what would you
   deliver at 10x the price? how would you still deliver at a tenth?).
5. **Trim & stack.** Cut high-cost/low-value items, keep low-cost/high-value
   (and high-cost/high-value if the price supports it). Favor one-to-many
   assets built once and delivered forever. That is exactly why software and
   templates get so valuable. Bundle the survivors, name each piece, assign
   each a believable dollar value, and present the stack against the price.

Sequencing rule: over-deliver like crazy at first ("create flow, monetize flow,
then add friction"), then systematize fulfillment with the cash.

## 4. Pricing psychology

- **Price-to-value divergence is the whole game.** People buy when perceived
  value clearly exceeds price. Never close the gap by cutting price. Grow
  value first, then raise price ("$100,000 of value for $10,000, money at a
  discount").
- **Premium pricing is a virtuous cycle:** higher price → more client
  investment → better results → better proof → more value → higher price.
  Cheap prices attract the worst clients and starve the margin you need to
  deliver an exceptional experience. In blind taste tests the expensive wine
  *is* better; price itself raises perceived value.
- Don't price against competitors: they're broke, and matching them means
  pricing for market efficiency (just-enough-to-survive). Aim so much higher
  that the buyer thinks "something entirely different must be going on here."
  That puts you in a category of one.
- Specialist positioning justifies premium (Luong): the Snapchat-ads-only
  agency charges full generalist retainers *because* it does one thing.
- Charge enough that it stings slightly. Invested customers do the work and
  get results. Guilt about high prices means you haven't built enough value or
  proof yet; "outwork your self-doubt."
- Continuity pricing knobs (Money Models): to steer buyers toward the monthly
  plan, price the one-time option 1.33x to 2.66x the monthly price (1.33x → ~50%
  choose continuity; 2.66x → ~90%). Bill every 4 weeks instead of monthly for
  13 cycles/year (+8.3% revenue, same words). Offer bulk-prepay discounts
  ("buy 5 months get 1 free") to pull cash forward.

## 5. Offer types and where each fits (Money Models)

Four offer types chain into a money model:

1. **Attraction offers** turn strangers into customers with free or discounted entry.
   - *Win Your Money Back*: pay now, get it back (as cash or credit) if you hit
     the goal or do the actions. Criteria must be easy to track, genuinely
     produce results, and advertise the business (posts, reviews, referrals).
     Apply winnings as credit spread over a longer, bigger package. Only run it
     if refund rate is already under ~5%.
   - *Giveaways, decoy offers, buy-X-get-Y-free, pay-less-now-or-more-later.*
   - Free trials and free tools live here: advertise the free thing, not the
     membership behind it.
2. **Upsell offers** raise spend: the Classic Upsell solves the customer's
   *next* problem the moment they become aware of it (sell the earmuff storage
   when they collect the free earmuffs).
3. **Downsell offers** turn "no" into "yes": payment plans, trials with a
   penalty, feature-reduced tiers.
4. **Continuity offers** keep people buying. Nobody subscribes cold; no
   successful continuity business sells a bare membership. Lead with a bonus
   worth more than the first payment ("want to know how to get this $1,000
   thing free? Join for $100/mo"), keep bonuses related to the core offer and
   made of things you already have, and trade bigger bonuses for longer
   commitments.

Design rule: attraction gets them in, upsell/downsell get cash today,
continuity builds tomorrow's revenue. Use all four.

**A mission is not an offer.** An ad needs a specific thing the reader gets
for clicking: an assessment, a free call, a seat at an event. One family
office ran "build lasting legacy" ads and got about 20,000 views, 600 clicks
and zero conversions. The fix was to make a free local wealth event the
offer, give the existing template as a bonus just for registering, and hand
it out in person so people have a reason to show up. A small fee ($50 or
$100) for the follow-up meeting filters out people who are not serious. (We're
Running Out of Referrals. Where Do Our Next Clients Come From?)

## 6. The 5-step killer offer structure (Tim Luong)

A lighter-weight ladder that complements the Grand Slam stack; good for a
landing page or pitch audit:

1. **Niche tagline / ICP**: so specific the right reader says "this is me."
2. **Specialist pricing**: charge generalist rates (or above) for the narrow
   thing, because you're the go-to.
3. **Benefits over features**: sell the dream outcome, not the mechanics
   ("double your follower count," not "we post daily").
4. **Why you**: proof, testimonials, credentials, your own results. In this
   era, overwhelming proof is what sells.
   Match proof to each type of buyer: one piece of feedback per topic or
   avatar, not one generic quote. No testimonials yet? Run a session on each
   topic and screenshot the feedback. (Will This Line Get More People to Buy?)
5. **Bonuses, guarantees, urgency/scarcity**: the push over the line.

Why offers fail (Luong): too generic (indistinguishable from 10,000
competitors) and too broad (five services, no identity). Blue-ocean positioning
can come purely from focus, even if a third of competitors technically offer
the same thing, being *only* that thing makes you look one-of-one.

## 7. The four enhancers

### Scarcity (limits *how many*)
Three types: limited seats/slots, limited-supply bonuses, never-available-again.
Service versions: total business cap, per-week client cap, cohort cap.
**Honest scarcity is the easiest:** you genuinely can't take 1,000 clients.
State your real capacity ("81% full this week") and let psychology work. Always
actually sell out, and announce the sell-out. Works on free things too: a
checklist limited to 20 downloads/week outperforms an always-available one.

### Urgency (limits *when*)
Four ethical patterns: cohort-based rolling urgency ("next group starts
Monday"), rolling seasonal promotions (same offer, new themed deadline each
month), pricing/bonus-based urgency (the *promotion* expires, not the service),
exploding opportunity (the arbitrage window itself is closing). Deadlines drive
decisions: 50 to 60% of launch sales land in the final hours.

### Bonuses
A single offer is less valuable than the same offer split into named parts and
stacked. Rules: always offer them; name each with a benefit; price-anchor each
and justify it (believably; fake $15,000 values destroy trust); tools,
checklists and templates beat extra trainings (lower effort = higher value);
each bonus should destroy a specific objection; total bonus value should
eclipse the core price. Never discount the core offer to close. Add a bonus
instead. Advanced: negotiate other businesses' products as bonuses (free
marketing for them, free value for you, often plus affiliate commission).

### Guarantees
Risk is the #1 objection; reversing it can lift conversion 2 to 4x. Power form:
"If you don't get X in Y time, we will Z."

**Guarantee picker:**

| Situation | Use | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Low-ticket B2C, strong product | Unconditional refund | 30-day no-questions money back |
| You know the actions that drive success | Conditional | "Do the calls + implement; hit X or money back" |
| High fulfillment cost | Conditional or anti-guarantee | Terms-based, or "all sales final because we expose our playbook" |
| Upselling an existing happy customer | Credit-based | Refund as credit toward what they already like |
| Outcome is measurable and trusted | Implied / performance | Revshare, pay-per-result, ratchets |
| Want maximum teeth without time risk | Service guarantee | "We keep working free until you get X" |
| Consumable / instantly copyable value | Anti-guarantee | "All sales final" + a credible reason why |

Stack guarantees (unconditional 30-day + conditional triple-money-back 90-day).
Name the guarantee vividly, not "satisfaction guarantee." Do the math before
fearing refunds: 30% more sales with double the refund rate is still +23% net.
Warning: a guarantee papering over a weak product just buys refunds.

## 8. Naming: the MAGIC formula

Use 3 to 5 of the components; punchy beats complete:

- **M**agnetic reason why (Free, 88% Off, Grand Opening, New Year)
- **A**vatar called out (Busy Moms, Salon Owners, Brooklyn Executives)
- **G**oal articulated (Pain Free, First Client, Double Your Profit)
- **I**nterval (21-Day, 6-Week; omit with quantified claims where platforms ban implied-guarantee durations)
- **C**ontainer word (Challenge, Blueprint, Bootcamp, Sprint, System, Reset)

Rhyme and alliteration help ("Six-Pack Fast Track"). Name every bonus and stack
item with the same formula.

**When offers fatigue,** change in this order (cheapest first): 1 ad creative →
2 body copy → 3 headline/wrapper (rename the same offer) → 4 duration →
5 free/discount component → 6 the monetization structure itself. Never rebuild
a converting offer out of boredom. Change the wrapping paper, not the bundle.

## 9. Offer audit checklist

Run any existing offer through this:

- [ ] Is the market painful, funded, targetable, growing?
- [ ] Does the headline name a specific avatar and dream outcome?
- [ ] All four value levers addressed: big outcome, proof it works for *them*,
      fast first win, minimal effort/sacrifice?
- [ ] Does it solve every perceived problem, or is one "what about eating out?"
      objection silently killing sales?
- [ ] Is it a stack of named components with anchored values, or one opaque blob?
- [ ] Is price set by value delivered, not competitor average?
- [ ] Is there a guarantee, and is it the right row of the picker table?
- [ ] At least one honest scarcity or urgency element?
- [ ] Bonuses that each kill a named objection?
- [ ] MAGIC-formula name that couldn't be confused with a competitor?
- [ ] What's the next offer? (Upsell ready for the moment their next problem
      appears; continuity for ongoing value.)
- [ ] Can you say the price out loud without cracking a smile? If it's easy,
      you're probably underpriced.

## 10. Worked micro-examples

### A. Free-tool lead magnet feeding a SaaS trial

Context: a free media-kit maker for creators, capturing emails, then nudging
into a brand-discovery app with 5 to 7 free lookups before the paywall.

- **The free tool is an attraction offer, so treat it like a real offer.** Name
  it with MAGIC ("Free 5-Minute Creator Media Kit Builder" beats "media kit
  tool"): magnetic reason (free), avatar (creators), goal (a kit brands take
  seriously), interval (5 minutes), container (builder). Value equation: dream
  outcome = looking professional to brands; likelihood = show sample kits from
  real creators; time delay = kit rendered before they leave; effort = import
  their stats automatically instead of a blank form.
- **The email is the price.** Justify it with a bonus, not a gate: "we'll email
  your kit as a shareable link + a pitch-email template" (a checklist-type
  bonus: low cost, high value, kills the "now what?" problem).
- **The bridge is a Classic Upsell:** solve the *next* problem the moment it
  appears. The moment their kit exists, their next problem is "which brands do
  I send it to?" That is the brand-discovery pitch, delivered right there.
- **The 5 to 7 free lookups are a trial-with-limits attraction offer.** Frame the
  cap as honest scarcity, and show the meter ("2 of 5 free lookups left").
  The countdown itself creates urgency without a fake timer.
- **At the paywall, stack, don't just gate:** unlimited lookups + saved lists +
  fresh-data alerts + the pitch-template pack (named, anchored). Consider a
  credit-based guarantee ("if your first month finds you no brand worth
  pitching, next month is free"). It is low-risk for software, kills the "will this
  work for my niche?" objection.
- **Continuity knobs:** annual at a discount vs monthly priced with the
  1.33x to 2.66x ratio depending on whether you want cash now or recurring base.

### B. Service business (contrast case)

A bookkeeping firm for Shopify stores: "Lakeway Shopify Sellers: Clean Books
in 21 Days (Done-For-You)." Stack = catch-up cleanup + monthly close + tax-time
pack + Slack-hours support. Conditional guarantee: "books closed by the 5th
every month or that month is free." Scarcity: onboarding cap of 4 stores/month
(true capacity). Upsell at the quarterly review: CFO-lite forecasting. Same
machinery, zero software.

## 11. Current tactics (web, 2026)

Sourced field findings; contradictions with the books flagged explicitly.

1. **Interactive free tools beat static lead magnets for SaaS.** A project
   management SaaS's ROI calculator converted its landing page at 34% vs 11%
   for the prior PDF guide, with 22% lead→trial conversion because calculator
   users self-qualify ([Citedy](https://www.citedy.com/blog/lead-magnet-ideas-25-free-tool-funnels-that-convert-high-intent-visitors)).
   Calculators work because they're immediately useful, personalized, and
   imply expertise ([Userlist](https://userlist.com/blog/lead-magnets-saas/)).
2. **Match the lead magnet to the ICP of the paid product.** A free tool that
   attracts the wrong avatar fills the funnel with people who'll never pay
   ([Wisepops](https://wisepops.com/blog/lead-magnets), consistent with
   Hormozi's "keep bonuses related to your core offer").
3. **Freemium converts at 2 to 5% typically; hybrid gating converts best.** One
   documented case went from 3.8% (feature-gating only) to 7.4% by combining
   feature gates + usage limits + personalized upgrade triggers
   ([American Impact Review](https://americanimpactreview.com/article/e2026022),
   [Monetizely](https://www.getmonetizely.com/articles/the-economics-of-freemium-when-do-free-users-actually-generate-revenue)).
   The lookup-cap-plus-locked-features pattern is the current best practice.
4. **Focus on upgrade-ready moments, not the whole free base.** Winning
   freemium teams obsess over the ~3% who *should* upgrade hitting a
   frictionless paywall at the moment of need
   ([Rework](https://resources.rework.com/libraries/saas-growth/free-to-paid-conversion)),
   the product-led version of the Classic Upsell.
5. **Usage-metered free tiers frame conversion as crossing a threshold**, not
   buying something new (AWS-style), which lowers the decision's perceived
   effort ([Software Pricing Partners](https://softwarepricing.com/blog/freemium-saas/)).
6. **Fake urgency now actively backfires.** Buyers refresh-test countdown
   timers; session-based fakes are detected and erode trust. One agency logged
   a 217% two-week conversion spike from manufactured urgency and killed the
   tactic because long-term trust metrics fell
   ([7thGrowth](https://7thgrowth.com/blog/the-fake-urgency-strategy-that-boosted-conversions-by-217-and-why-we-stopped-doing-it/),
   [Growth Suite](https://www.growthsuite.net/blog/real-urgency-vs-fake-urgency-why-your-countdown-timers-are-not-working)).
   **Contradiction flag:** Hormozi's urgency chapter is directionally
   compatible ("make sure the dates are real… or you'll look like every other
   wannabe marketer") but the 2026 evidence is stronger than the book implies:
   merchants using both real and fake scarcity convert *worse* than those
   using neither. Treat "manufactured" seasonal-promo urgency as riskier today
   than the book suggests; tie every deadline to something verifiably real.
7. **Real countdown timers still work:** authentic deadline timers lift
   conversions 8 to 14% (up to ~40% well-targeted) and genuine timers outperform
   fake ones 2 to 3x on long-term metrics
   ([Growth Suite](https://www.growthsuite.net/blog/do-countdown-timers-still-work-in-2025),
   [LiquidBoost](https://liquidboost.app/blog/countdown-timer-conversion-data)).
8. **Regulatory risk is now concrete:** the FTC's dark-patterns report names
   false urgency and fake low-stock messages as deceptive, with enforcement
   actions against fabricated timers
   ([Growth Suite](https://www.growthsuite.net/resources/shopify-countdown-timer/common-mistakes)).
   The books treat ethics as reputation advice; today it's also legal exposure.
9. **The value equation is being applied to AI/SaaS offers as ROI-speed +
   onboarding-friction reduction + strong guarantees**, with the "decrease the
   bottom" strategy dominating current SaaS positioning
   ([Blockchain.news AI analysis](https://blockchain.news/ainews/ai-driven-value-proposition-optimization-with-hormozi-s-grand-slam-offer-formula-boosting-conversions-and-business-impact),
   [QuantumByte](https://quantumbyte.ai/articles/alex-hormozi-value-equation-app-monetization)).
10. **Pay-only-if-successful structures are mainstream in education/bootcamp
    offers** ("don't pay unless hired in 90 days"), with implied guarantees moving
    down-market from B2B services
    ([mrusbooksnreviews summary of current examples](https://mrusbooksnreviews.com/alex-hormozi-grand-slam-offers/)).

## 12. Quick-start: crafting a new offer in one sitting

1. Pick the niche; write the ICP tagline (Section 2, 6).
2. Run the 5-step Grand Slam stack (Section 3).
3. Price high against value, not competitors (Section 4).
4. Pick a guarantee from the picker (Section 7).
5. Add one honest scarcity + one real-deadline urgency element (Section 7, 11).
6. Stack and name bonuses that kill the top 3 objections (Section 7).
7. Name it with MAGIC (Section 8).
8. Decide the next offer in the chain: upsell and continuity (Section 5).
9. Audit with the checklist (Section 9). Ship, then iterate the wrapper, never
   the working machine (Section 8).
