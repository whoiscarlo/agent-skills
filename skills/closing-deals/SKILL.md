# Closing Deals

## Overview

Moves for the deal itself. Core rule: **never move the price alone.** If price moves, terms move with it. If cash is the problem, change when you get paid before you change what you sell.

Siblings: offer-crafting (building the offer), sales-psychology (objections, proof placement, framing), business-diagnosis (finding the real constraint), lead-gen-funnels (getting leads in).

## Discount requests: trade, don't cut

1. **Find why they said no:** value, details, budget, authority or timing. Some no's are final.
2. **Asked to go cheaper, say you can do it for more.** Some then buy at the quoted price.
3. **Loop on money first.** Why can't you afford it? How important is this? What does not doing it cost you?
4. **Lower price means less product.** Take out the guarantee for $1,000 off, or cut a session. Same product at two prices is unfair, and customers talk.

## Three prices instead of one

- **Basic:** the lowest price you would happily accept. Your safety net.
- **Advanced:** basic plus a few extras. The one you want picked. Mark it "most popular".
- **Expensive:** the shot in the sky. It anchors everything below.
- Even two tiers help: a $167 option with a call next to a $99 product got about 30% to pay more.

## Negotiation leverage

- **Build leverage before the meeting:** other bids, offers or customers. Doing nothing can be a strong alternative. Only accept a deal that beats it.
- **Keep your other offer private.** Reveal a $10 offer and you get about $10.25. Keep it quiet and ask $15.
- **Put three offers of similar value down at once.** Lower fee with a longer commitment; higher fee with premium support; pay as you go at slightly higher rates. All must work for you. "All three work for me. Which one is better for you?" Their pick shows if they care most about price, service or freedom.

## Enterprise: giant price, then fix cash timing

- **Say the biggest number you can think of** and watch the reaction. You can go down, never up. Quote $250K, hear "okay", and you may have left $500K behind.
- **Annoying terms mean the price is too low.** Ask what price would make agreeing to all their conditions easy.
- **Close the cash gap with terms:** setup or onboarding fees up front; "pay your first two months up front, get two months free at the end"; or first and last month now, then monthly; or a small paid 90 day engagement that upsells into the main contract.
- Keep small, fast deals as a bridge.

## Show rate is a scheduling problem

Across about 4,000 appointments a day, the top lever was availability.

1. More slots, seven days a week.
2. 15 minute start times, not 60. The call can still run 45 or 60 minutes.
3. Precise starts like 3:05.
4. Book close to the conversation. Same day beats 2 to 4 days out.
5. Drag appointments forward: offer a slot that "just opened" today.

## Proof without names

- Tell the story with a stand-in name, specific on before and after. Say names were changed for privacy.
- Show present proof: play real calls you generate now, then ask, "Can you handle 10 to 15 of these a week?"
- Remove risk. A free trial asks the buyer to trust nothing.

## The upsell honesty test

Does the customer get exactly what you said they would? Then offering it is a service. If it relies on an invented problem, drop it or build something real.

## Prepay, free users, no sales team

- **Diagnose, then let the math price it.** 200 pounds to 140 at 2 a week is 30 weeks, plus about 16 of maintenance: 46 weeks at $200. Offer "save 20" to prepay, "save 10" for half down. On a no, seesaw: most up front, smallest monthly. Only prepayers get the guarantee.
- **Free users, no revenue:** add a call step to onboarding. Five signups a day is five sales calls. Make free less complete.
- **No sales team** works only when offer and brand carry the selling. Qualified buyers want price and contents, then decide in about two minutes.

## Common mistakes

- Vague "negotiate shorter terms" for enterprise instead of a giant price plus prepay and credit.
- Blaming lead quality for no-shows before checking availability, slot size and booking distance.
- Negotiating with no alternatives, or revealing the other offer.
- Asking what they value instead of letting a choice of offers show it.
- Discounting the same product instead of removing a named part.
- Telling a CEO a story instead of numbers.
- Building more for free users nobody has called.
