# Business Diagnosis Glossary

Trimmed from the author's video study notes. Examples use Laurianne's numbers from "I Do $30M a Year".

## Unit economics

- **CAC (customer acquisition cost):** ad spend divided by new customers. $150,000 on ads for 100 buyers is a $1,500 CAC.
- **LTV (lifetime value):** everything one customer pays over the whole relationship: first purchase plus every upsell and renewal. The playbooks note treats it as lifetime gross profit, so subtract delivery cost when you know it.
- **LTV to CAC ratio:** dollars a customer brings in per dollar spent to get them. 3 to 1 is the usual healthy target. $2,400 / $1,500 is 1.6 to 1, too thin.
- **AOV (average order value):** average cash collected on one purchase. Payment plans drag it down because only the first payment counts. A $2,000 offer with a $200 a month option gave about $900.
- **ROAS (return on ad spend):** revenue per ad dollar. $10,000 in ads earning $30,000 is 3x. Cutting spend usually raises ROAS, because you keep only the cheapest buyers.
- **Churn:** the share of paying customers who cancel each month.
- **Gross margin:** what is left of revenue after the direct cost to deliver.

## Offers and funnel

- **Front end:** the first, usually cheaper, thing a new customer buys.
- **Back end:** the higher-priced offer sold to people who already bought.
- **Ascension rate (upgrade rate):** percent of front-end buyers who move up. 70 of 1,000 is 7%. Hormozi wants 25% to 50%.
- **Continuity:** a recurring offer, like a monthly membership.
- **Self-liquidating offer:** a cheap product sold before the main offer, priced so its sales pay back the ad cost.
- **Through line:** a forced path to the next step, such as ending the onboarding call only once the next call is booked.
- **Activation point:** the early action after which customers tend to stay.
- **Qualifier:** a question that filters out people who cannot buy, like business ownership or revenue level.

## Sales and cash

- **Setter:** qualifies leads and books meetings. Does not close.
- **Closer:** runs the final call and asks for the money.
- **BNPL (buy now, pay later):** third-party financing that pays you most of the price now and collects installments itself.
- **Cash flow:** money in versus money out over time. You can be profitable on paper and still run out of cash.
- **Constraint (bottleneck):** the one weakest point limiting the whole business. Fixing anything else does little.
- **CAT to LTGP:** cost of acquiring talent against lifetime gross profit per employee. Used to measure reps like customers.
