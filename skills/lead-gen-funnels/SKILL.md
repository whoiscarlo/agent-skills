# Lead-Gen Funnels Playbook

Distilled from Alex Hormozi's *$100M Leads* (2023), Russell Brunson's *Lead Funnels*
and *DotCom Secrets*, plus 2025-26 web benchmarks (cited inline). This is a build
playbook, not a summary — work top to bottom when designing a funnel: offer →
magnet → pages → follow-up → metrics → referrals.

Siblings: `offer-crafting` (what you sell and how it's framed), `conversion-copy`
(the words on the pages), `sales-psychology` (why the levers work),
`ux-persuasion` (page-level interaction design), `business-diagnosis` (unit
economics, pricing, churn), `closing-deals` (discounts, pricing tiers,
negotiation). This skill owns the *structure*.

## Mental model

- A **lead** is someone with the problem and the money. An **engaged lead** has
  raised their hand (opted in, replied, booked). Funnels manufacture engaged
  leads; sales converts them. Design for the raised hand, not the sale (Hormozi).
- Advertise your core offer first — if people buy directly, you don't need a
  funnel yet. Add a lead magnet when the offer is expensive or considered.
- A **lead magnet** is a complete solution to a narrow problem that, once solved,
  reveals the next problem — which your paid offer solves. Hormozi's bar-pretzel
  test: pretzels solve hunger, reveal thirst, and the drink pays for the pretzels.
- **The list is the asset.** The funnel exists to (1) build the list, (2) build a
  relationship with it, (3) recoup ad cost fast, (4) ascend buyers up a value
  ladder (Brunson).

## Funnel anatomy (the 5-part spine)

Every lead funnel has these parts, in order (Brunson, *Lead Funnels*):

1. **Lead magnet** — attracts the ideal customer and delivers real value.
2. **Squeeze/landing page** — trades the magnet for an email address. An explicit
   barter; nothing else happens on this page.
3. **Thank-you page** — fulfills the promise immediately (builds trust), then
   presents exactly one next step.
4. **Sales page** *(optional at first)* — recoups ad cost and starts the buyer
   list. Skip it early; point the thank-you page at your content channel instead.
5. **Follow-up sequence** — builds the relationship and moves people up the
   ladder. Most of the money is here, not on the pages.

## Lead magnet design (Hormozi's 7 steps)

1. **Pick a narrow, meaningful problem** whose solution reveals a problem your
   core offer solves. (Selling homes? Magnet = "what is my house worth," not
   "how to sell a home.")
2. **Pick a solution type** — there are three:
   - **Reveal their problem** ("diagnosis"): speed test, audit, score, analysis.
     Best when the problem worsens with time.
   - **Sample or trial**: limited uses or limited time on the real product. Best
     when the core offer is a recurring solution to a recurring problem.
   - **One step of a multi-step process**: give a genuinely complete step free,
     sell the rest.
3. **Pick a delivery vehicle** — software/tool, information, done-for-you
   service, or physical item. 3 types x 4 vehicles = up to 12 magnets for one
   problem; build several and rotate.
4. **Test the name.** Headline > image > subheadline, in that order of leverage.
   Small wording changes swing results ("How to get strangers to want to buy
   your stuff" beat the same line without "how to"). Poll your audience before
   building.
5. **Make it easy to consume** — every format you add (video + text + audio +
   mobile) multiplies take-rate; Hormozi attributes 2-4x lift to format coverage.
6. **Make it darn good** — "give away the secrets, sell the implementation."
   The free thing should feel worth paying for; 99% won't buy but all of them
   set your reputation.
7. **Make it easy to say they want more** — end with one CTA: what to do + a
   real reason to do it now.

### Lead magnet quality bar (ship checklist)

- [ ] Solves one narrow problem *completely* — a real result, not a teaser
- [ ] Good enough that you could charge for it ("so good they'd feel stupid saying no")
- [ ] Solving it naturally reveals the next problem your paid offer solves
- [ ] First win arrives in minutes, not days (Eben Pagan's "Kiss Test" was 5 sentences)
- [ ] Title is specific and curiosity-driven, aimed at pain, not category-generic
      ("The 12 Biggest Mistakes Network Marketers Make" beats "Marketing Guide")
- [ ] Consumable in multiple formats / on a phone
- [ ] Ends with one clear CTA plus a genuine reason to act now
- [ ] Unit economics checked: even with a delivery cost, it should *lower*
      blended CAC. Hormozi's math: a $25-to-deliver free magnet that triples
      engagement cut cost-per-customer from $3,000 to $1,000.

### Magnets for cold outbound

A big cold list with no magnet asks strangers for a sale. (I Send 100,000
Emails a Month. What Should I Give Away?)

- **Give away what others charge for**, picked from what the buyer wants now
  (for B2B tech CEOs, credibility). A podcast guest spot works and doubles
  as an hour with the prospect.
- **Free can be one task, not a trial period**, or a guarantee: "five pieces
  in these publications by this date, or the PR is free."
- **Split the list across ideas** and keep the best response rate. About
  four email cycles finds the winner. Any of them beats nothing.

## Squeeze page anatomy checklist

Brunson's three cardinal rules, in priority order:

1. **Keep it simple** — headline, image of the magnet, opt-in form. No nav, no
   competing links, no video unless tested in.
2. **Build curiosity** — the headline states a problem + a hook that can't be
   resolved without opting in.
3. **Tell them what to do** — literal instruction: "Enter your email and I'll
   send it right away."

Element checklist:
- [ ] Headline: problem + specific curiosity hook (pain-first beats pleasure-first)
- [ ] Visual of the deliverable (make the free thing look like a product)
- [ ] Opt-in form: **email only**. Field-count data: 1 field converts 4.41%,
      2 fields 2.90%, 3 fields 1.93% ([Opensend](https://www.opensend.com/post/lead-capture-rate-statistics-ecommerce))
- [ ] If you need more fields, use a multi-step form — email first, details after
      (multi-step 5.64% vs 3.07% single-step, same source)
- [ ] One CTA button with instruction-verb copy
- [ ] Expectation line: what they get, when, and that you'll email them
- [ ] Built fast (minutes, not days) — test before polishing

> **Book-vs-web contradiction — plan with modern numbers.** Brunson says a good
> landing page converts 20-30% and cites his own 55.63% page. Current data:
> lead-gen landing pages typically convert 1-5%, 6-7% is strong
> ([Leadpages](https://leadpages.com/blog/landing-page-conversion-benchmarks-2026));
> average email-capture across 1B popup displays is 4.65%, top decile ~19.8%
> ([Opensend](https://www.opensend.com/post/lead-capture-rate-statistics-ecommerce)).
> Brunson's figures reflect warm traffic sent to a pure curiosity offer.
> Dedicated opt-in pages fed by warm/owned traffic can still hit 20-40%; cold
> paid traffic will not. Set targets by traffic temperature.

## Thank-you page rules

- Deliver the magnet **on the page** (download/link) *and* by email. Never
  bait-and-switch ("buy this to get what you signed up for" destroys the trust
  the funnel just built).
- Exactly two jobs: fulfill the promise, then present **one** next step —
  a low-ticket offer, a webinar registration, or (if you have nothing to sell
  yet) a subscribe CTA for your podcast/channel.
- This is the highest-attention moment you will ever get with this lead; the
  first 48 hours form the lasting impression (Hormozi). Stack a fast win here.

## Value ladder mapper

Map every funnel onto an ascent before building pages (Brunson, Secret #2):

| Rung | Price | Job | Fill in |
|---|---|---|---|
| Bait | Free | Attract dream customer, start trust | lead magnet / free tool |
| Frontend | $ (low) | Turn lead into *buyer*, recoup ad cost | tripwire, book, starter plan |
| Middle | $$ | Core offer | main product/service |
| Backend | $$$ | Highest value, highest touch | premium tier, done-for-you |
| Continuity | recurring | Residual income — "no value ladder is complete without it" | subscription, membership |

Rules of thumb:
- Most businesses have one rung. Growth usually comes from adding the *front*
  (attractive free/low entry) and the *back* (premium ascent), not fixing the middle.
- A buyer is worth pursuing upward immediately: the frontend's job is to create
  buyers, not profit. Brunson's One-Hundred-Visitor Test: $197 direct offer
  converted 1%; free-plus-shipping converted 8% with 25% taking the $197 upsell
  — roughly 2x revenue and 7-8x more buyers on the list. An order-form bump
  added ~34% take-rate with near-zero effort.
- "Free" is a category of its own, not a price point (Ariely's chocolate
  experiment): dropping from 1 cent to free changes behavior discontinuously.

## Follow-up sequence skeleton (day 0 / 1 / 3 / 7)

Merges Brunson's 5-email Soap Opera Sequence with current timing data. Each
email opens a loop the next one closes.

- **Day 0, within 5 minutes** — deliver the magnet, set expectations ("one email
  a day this week"), open a loop ("tomorrow I'll send you X — watch for the
  subject line '...'"). Welcome emails sent within 5 minutes get ~3x the opens
  of next-morning sends ([MailerToGo](https://resources.mailertogo.com/statistics/trial-to-paid-conversion-email-statistics-2025)).
- **Day 1 — high drama + backstory.** Start the story at the crisis point, not
  the beginning; end stuck at the wall your reader is at now. Open the next loop.
- **Day 2-3 — epiphany.** The turn that led to your solution; first real link
  to the core offer.
- **Day 4-5 — hidden benefits.** Second-order payoffs they haven't considered;
  parable format, soft CTA.
- **Day 6-7 — urgency + CTA.** One last push with a *real* deadline (price
  change, cohort close, limited seats). "Fake urgency will backfire on you, and
  you'll lose all credibility" (Brunson).

After the sequence, move people to broadcasts — Brunson's "Seinfeld" style:
90% entertainment/story, 10% content, and **every email ties back to an offer**.
He advocates daily email; treat cadence as something you earn — watch
unsubscribe and spam-complaint rates rather than adopting daily by default.

For product/trial follow-up specifically: behavioral triggers beat calendar
drips (20-40% lift over generic sequences), and each email should push **one**
activation action — day-one activators convert at 3-5x
([MailerToGo](https://resources.mailertogo.com/statistics/trial-to-paid-conversion-email-statistics-2025)).

### CTA construction (Hormozi)

Every CTA = **what to do** (clear, direct verb: "call now", "grab your copy") +
**a reason to do it now**: real scarcity (advertise your actual capacity
limits), real urgency (actual deadlines), or simply any stated "because…" —
reasons increase compliance even when weak, but honest ones work best and
compound trust.

## Lead quality: qualifiers vs sales motion

"Bad leads" and "low close rate" have two opposite fixes. Diagnose before
changing the ads.

- **Too little friction, wrong people on calls.** Buyers agree on price but
  cannot pay the first installment or get financing. That is a lead problem.
  Add real qualifiers to the form and the lead magnet: facts like "do you own
  a business" and revenue level. "Are you willing to invest?" filters
  nothing. Let the thank-you page sort: qualified leads go to sales, the rest
  to an automated path, maybe a low-priced offer. Expect cost per lead to
  rise; each call is worth more. (Should I Add Qualifiers If My Close Rate Is
  Low?)
- **Good leads, no selling in between.** The leads own homes and want the
  service, yet do not buy. That is a sales motion problem, not lead quality.
  "Click and buy" is too little friction for a high-end offer; a hundred
  forms is too much. Add the right selling steps, and check whether the
  price fits the best customers. (My Paid Leads Are Low Quality. What Do I
  Do?)
- **Leads came in, nobody worked them.** "Buy a membership" gives no reason
  to act today. Run a $19 to $99 front end offer built on the most common
  pain, like a screening, then prescribe the core offer as the fix. Put it
  on the thank-you page with a scheduler; people who pay tend to show up.
  Call every other lead. One trained setter (the person who calls and books
  leads) can be the top priority. (My Ads Brought Leads but Nobody Signed
  Up. What Now?)
- **Crowded market, cheap bad leads.** Raise the price (about $2K or more)
  and add friction so only serious buyers reach the offer. (What's the Best
  Offer to Get Wedding Photographer Clients?)
- **Lead source ranking** (same source): referrals, then content, then paid
  search. Display network leads are the worst.
- **Build the avatar from your best customers**: who spent most and stayed
  longest. Name their traits in ad call outs, forms and pages.

## Referral flywheel

Referrals are the cheapest, highest-LTV leads, and the only channel that
compounds (customer brings two, two bring four). Two failure causes: the
product isn't remarkable, and nobody asks.

- **Goodwill = value received minus price paid.** Referrals are a function of
  goodwill x asking.
- **Growth equation: referral rate vs churn rate.** Referrals > churn →
  compounding growth without ads. Referrals < churn → you advertise to stand still.
- Earn goodwill six ways: sell better-fit customers; promise less and
  overdeliver; find what your best customers do and make everyone do it;
  engineer faster/more frequent wins (never leave them not knowing what happens
  next); keep removing effort from the product; keep selling the next thing.
- **Ask like it's an offer** — show what the referrer gets. Seven proven asks:
  1. One-sided: pay your CAC to the referrer (or discount the friend)
  2. Two-sided (Dropbox 39x'd in 15 months; PayPal to 1M users): split CAC
     between referrer credit and friend discount
  3. Ask at the moment of purchase ("who else would you do this with? People
     who join with someone get ~3x the results")
  4. Referral as negotiation chip (discount in exchange for 3 live intros)
  5. Time-boxed referral events ("bring-a-friend" promotions, 1-4 weeks)
  6. Always-on program mentioned in all content/ads
  7. Unlockable non-cash bonuses (status, features, service tiers) for
     referral + testimonial
- Ask for a real three-way introduction (text/email/call), not "names and numbers."

## Traffic in one paragraph (the core four)

Warm/cold audience x one-to-one/one-to-many gives the only four ways to
advertise: **warm outreach, posting content, cold outreach, paid ads**. Scale
any of them with "more, better, new" — more volume, better creative, then new
placements → new platforms → new methods, in that order. Beyond personal
effort: lead getters — customers (referrals, above), employees, agencies,
affiliates. Channel tactics are out of scope here; this skill owns what
happens after the click.

**Content is now ads** (What's the Best Offer to Get Wedding Photographer
Clients?): post lots, then run the pieces that did well as ads. With little
traffic, send people to a live call; with more, a live webinar.

**When referrals run out** (We're Running Out of Referrals. Where Do Our Next
Clients Come From?): your contact list is finite. Pick one path to strangers:
ads to a webinar (needs the most skill), local ads to an in-person event
(easiest; higher cost per lead, higher conversion), or referral partners
such as attorneys and accountants who already serve your buyers. Clicks with
zero conversions point at the offer and the page, not the audience: the ad
must offer a concrete thing to get. Event funnel math from that note: of 100
confirms, about 25 show up, about half book a consult, about half of those
close, so roughly 6 clients. Nurture hard between confirm and event.

## Funnel metrics to instrument

Wire these in before launch; each stage needs a counter and a conversion pair.

| Stage | Metric | Reference points |
|---|---|---|
| Page → email | Opt-in rate | Cold lead-gen pages 1-5%, 6-7% strong ([Leadpages](https://leadpages.com/blog/landing-page-conversion-benchmarks-2026)); popups avg 4.65%, top ~19.8% ([Opensend](https://www.opensend.com/post/lead-capture-rate-statistics-ecommerce)); warm-traffic dedicated squeeze pages 20-40% |
| Magnet | Consumption/completion rate | If they don't consume it, nothing downstream works — instrument it |
| Email | Open / click rates | Welcome + trial emails run 30-45% opens ([MailerToGo](https://resources.mailertogo.com/statistics/trial-to-paid-conversion-email-statistics-2025)) |
| Free → trial/paid step | Trial start rate, then trial→paid | Opt-in (no card) trials ~8.9-18.2%; card-required 31-49%; median B2B ~15-17% ([Userpilot](https://userpilot.com/blog/saas-average-conversion-rate/), [Meisa](https://meisa.io/learn/trial-conversion-benchmarks)) |
| Economics | Cost per engaged lead, blended CAC, LTGP:CAC | Hormozi's floor: LTGP:CAC ≥ 3:1; lead magnet should *cut* CAC, not add to it |
| Payback | Days to recoup ad spend per lead | Brunson targets day 1-5 breakeven via frontend offer |
| Flywheel | Referral rate vs monthly churn | Referrals > churn = compounding |

Treat Brunson's "$1/month per list name" as motivation, not a planning number —
it's era- and niche-specific.

## Current tactics (web, 2026)

1. **Free interactive tools are the top-performing magnet class.** Submagic's
   AI utility tools took it to 150K daily visitors in 10 months with $300K ARR
   attributed to the funnel; another company's calculator suite drives 70% of
   inbound pipeline ([Citedy](https://www.citedy.com/blog/lead-magnet-ideas-25-free-tool-funnels-that-convert-high-intent-visitors)).
   Tools convert because output is personalized and instant — the strongest
   modern form of Hormozi's "reveal their problem" diagnosis magnet.
2. **"Engineering as marketing"**: build the magnet out of your product's core
   capability so the magnet *is* a product demo ([Founderpath](https://founderpath.com/blog/free-tools-as-a-lead-magnet),
   [Userlist](https://userlist.com/blog/lead-magnets-saas/)). Agrees with
   Hormozi's sample/trial type; extends it — the tool sells the product without a sales page.
3. **Offering any incentive roughly doubles capture** (+155% desktop, +102%
   mobile vs bare "join our newsletter") ([Opensend](https://www.opensend.com/post/lead-capture-rate-statistics-ecommerce)).
4. **Every form field is expensive**: 4.41% → 2.90% → 1.93% for 1/2/3 fields;
   multi-step forms recover the data without the drop (same source). *Contradicts
   nothing in the books but quantifies Brunson's "keep it simple."*
5. **Average capture is rising** (4.01% 2024 → 4.65% 2025) but the spread
   between average and top performers (~20%) is the real story — targeting and
   offer relevance, not widget choice (same source).
6. **Speed-to-first-email is a conversion lever**: 5-minute welcome = 3x opens;
   day-one product activation = 3-5x eventual conversion ([MailerToGo](https://resources.mailertogo.com/statistics/trial-to-paid-conversion-email-statistics-2025)).
7. **Structured trial sequences convert 8-15% vs 3-5% without** — the follow-up
   funnel is still where the money is, exactly as the books claim (same source).
8. **Behavioral triggers beat calendar drips by 20-40%** ([UsTechAutomations](https://ustechautomations.com/resources/blog/saas-trial-conversion-automation-double-rate)).
   *Partial contradiction*: Brunson's fixed day-1..day-5 Soap Opera is a
   calendar drip. Keep his story arc; fire the sends off behavior where you can
   (signed up, used tool, hit limit) rather than pure elapsed time.
9. **Dated book tactics — skip**: *Lead Funnels*' Facebook Messenger lists,
   desktop push lists, and auto-scraping social profiles from emails predate
   platform-policy and privacy changes; treat those chapters as history. The
   durable channel stack today is email + in-product messages + retargeting.
10. **Landing-page benchmark inflation in the books** — see the flagged
    contradiction in the squeeze-page section; always segment expectations by
    traffic temperature.

## Worked example: free media-kit maker → SaaS trial

Generic pattern: an interactive tool that produces a customized deliverable,
traded for an email, delivered via email + hosted link, nudging into a trial of
5-7 free uses before a paywall. Mapping onto the frameworks:

- **Magnet type**: sample/trial x software delivery (Hormozi type 2 + vehicle 1).
  Add a "reveal their problem" layer by scoring the result (completeness grade,
  benchmark vs peers) — diagnosis magnets create the itch the paid tier scratches.
- **Quality bar**: the free kit must be genuinely usable — send-to-a-brand good,
  not a watermark-crippled teaser. The narrow problem: "I need a presentable
  media kit today." The revealed problem: keeping it current, more looks,
  more pages — the subscription.
- **Capture placement**: let them build first, ask at the *value moment* —
  "enter your email to receive your kit" after the preview exists. Email-only,
  one field. Sunk effort + visible deliverable puts this gate well above
  cold-page benchmarks; instrument builder-start → preview → email as separate steps.
- **Thank-you page**: hosted link immediately + emailed copy (email doubles as
  deliverability test and habit anchor). One next step: "make your next one" or
  "customize further" — the activation action, not a hard sell.
- **Value ladder**: free kit (bait) → trial: 5-7 free uses (frontend, priced in
  attention) → subscription (core) → premium templates/team features (backend).
  The use-meter is *honest* scarcity — surface it ("2 of 7 kits left") per
  Hormozi's ethical-scarcity rule.
- **Follow-up skeleton**: Day 0 deliver + one-line expectation set; Day 1 story
  of why the tool exists (drama → backstory); Day 3 hidden benefits (capabilities
  they haven't used — behavioral: only send features they *haven't* touched);
  Day 5-7 use-meter urgency + upgrade CTA. Then monthly broadcast cadence.
  Behavioral overrides: hitting the use limit fires the upgrade email
  immediately regardless of day.
- **Metrics**: tool starts, completion rate, email capture at delivery gate,
  kits per user, limit-hit rate, limit-hit → paid rate (compare against the
  opt-in trial benchmark of ~9-18%), day-1 second-use rate (activation proxy).
- **Referral hook**: the deliverable is shareable by design — every hosted kit
  is a landing page for the tool. Add a two-sided ask at the limit: "give a
  friend 2 free kits, get 2 more yourself" (Dropbox pattern, asked at the
  moment of highest engagement).

## Anti-patterns

- Teaser magnets that withhold the solution ("sucky fluff" — actively destroys
  reputation with the 99% who never buy)
- Asking for name/company/phone on the first form
- Bait-and-switch thank-you pages
- Fake countdown timers and false scarcity
- Building one "perfect" magnet for a month instead of several fast variants
  and testing names
- Optimizing the squeeze page while the follow-up sequence doesn't exist
- Running a referral program with a leaky product (referrals < churn)
